In terms of other issues, I found that the mounts for my nema on the turret were loose. The bolts were too long and there was a gap between the motor and the tower. That was fixed by quickly printing four 1mm shims and putting them into the holes.

Overall, these actions saved me about 15 hours of reprinting.

Assembling the rest was fairly trivial.

![image](https://stasis.hackclub-assets.com/images/1777656370937-n0k6xr.png)

_Time spent: 5.533333333333333h_

![image](https://stasis.hackclub-assets.com/images/1773897913043-0tzi29.png)

Right now it's in a state where ~80% of the 3d cad is done. I'll talk about my next steps near the end of this entry.

My first issue when I sat down at my laptop today was how the fuck I was going to package everything.


![image](https://stasis.hackclub-assets.com/images/1773898057040-5pkfup.png)

I was immediately hit with a dillema: figure out how to packaghe this (comparatively) gigantic Nema 17 on my turret, or increase the size of my pulley.

I opted against option 2 because of three reasons:

1. Increasing the size would massively increase print time (my 90mm pulley is already a 4 hour print)
2. Increasing the pulley size would screw up my steps to degrees calculations (forcing me to not use whole numbers for the lidar math)
3. Keeping the same pulley size and adding a wider "brim" would lead to the same issue as reason 1.

I already had experience packaging tight things in FRC (much to the dismay of our assembly team), and figuring this out would provide a nice mental challenge for myself.

---
In true avoidant fasion, I did neither of the two options (although I was already set on #1). I actually started with the yoke first, so that I could figure out how much space I needed for my supports.

![image](https://stasis.hackclub-assets.com/images/1773898436548-cfr0cb.png)

The yoke was modelled to fit the TF-Luna lidar sensor.

By the way, for some reason the tf-luna uses M2 bolts for mounting. Do you know how fucking hard it is to find M2 bolts?? Home depot doesn't have them. Rona/Lowes doesn't have them. The weird speciality stores don't have them. I'm not dropping $10 on a 50 pack just for two fucking bolts.

THANKFULLY, I have a contact who regularly orders hardware from Fastenal, and I can tack on my M2 bolts on the purchase order for a few cents each.

On the walls of the yoke are two bearing holes. After deliberation I decided to put them on the yoke directly. If they were on the towers of my turret, then the yoke plastic could bend and deflect downwards. A bearing has higher surface area, and is much less likely to deflect the 3dp walls of the yoke. If the tower mounts were to deflect, then the yoke would remain rigid.

This is a really fancy way of saying that putting them on yoke makes it easier to keep the assembly stiff👍

---

I hit my first problem here. I was originally planning on using a 20T pinion pulley with a 36T deadaxle yoke pulley (deadaxle = mounted directly to the face instead of transmitting through the axle). The problem I hit was that when I added the hex pockets for my M3 nuts, they actually conflicted with the flange of my bearing.

The solution was to update my pulley sizes. I went from 20T-36T to 25T-45T. The 45T pulley was wide enough for the deadaxle mounting bolts to not conflict with the bearing flange while still allowing for an exact 1.8:1 ratio. (Why 1.8:1? With that ratio then one step on the Nema (1.8°) is transformed into exactly 1° on the driven pulley -> this simplifies my lidar math.)

---

With the yoke cadded in, I could then continue with my actual packaging. With the given pulley ratios, I used Recalc and found that an 85T belt would give me a center to center distance that was sensible in terms of turret height (sensible = good vibes, I'm vibecading. [not ai, but cading based on vibes])

I was in a trance when I made this. None of the fillets are standardized. Extrusion lengths were based on vibes. Holes were placed by throwing darts at my screen. It's beautiful.

I pocketed my turret pulley to allow for easy alignment of the towers. I then had to connect a bottom "foundation", a Nema mounting plate, and an axle holder all in one continuous part.
